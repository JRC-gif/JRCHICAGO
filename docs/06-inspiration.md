# Inspiration

All three references treat a website as an object you play with, not a brochure.

## papertoilet.com (Rafaël Rozendaal, 2006)

- **What it is:** A single roll of toilet paper you can spin and unroll. That's the entire site.
- **Take:** One absurd, tactile interaction that *is* the whole idea. No explanation needed; you get it by touching it.
- **Leave:** The total absence of information. Our site still has to show work and drive inquiries.

## Cookie Clicker

- **What it is:** An idle game where clicking a cookie earns cookies, which buy upgrades that earn more cookies.
- **Take:** Interaction that rewards staying. Escalation, small surprises, a reason to click "one more time." Dwell time by design.
- **Leave:** The grind and UI clutter. The payoff should arrive in seconds, not hours.

## Jeff Koons (jeffkoons.com)

- **What it is:** Balloon Dog, mirror-polished stainless steel, high-gloss color at monumental scale.
- **Take:** Art-world confidence. Oversized, glossy, reflective imagery. Very little text, and the text that exists is sure of itself.
- **Leave:** Anything that reads as a copy of his work. We borrow the attitude and the finish, not the objects.

## How they combine for Junior Chicago

A homepage with one signature, tactile interaction (papertoilet) that escalates or rewards play (Cookie Clicker), rendered in bold, glossy, oversized visuals with minimal copy (Koons). The work and the inquiry form are always one scroll or one tap away.
